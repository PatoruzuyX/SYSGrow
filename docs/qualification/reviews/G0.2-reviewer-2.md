3.2 G02-02 — Strict inhibition and minimum atomic admission
HOLD — High
Pinned evidence:

REMEDIATION.md L85–89 · REMEDIATION.md L122–161 · FOUNDATION_BASELINE.md L34–65.

The original atomic-admission dependency problem is corrected. R1b now defaults to strict inhibition, and its alternative implementation explicitly cannot lift R1a restrictions.

However, independent baseline inspection identifies an additional containment acceptance gap.
New finding: G0.2-SS-01 — Physical side effects outside command admission
Severity: High

The containment tests principally assert that denied paths produce zero adapter command submissions. This is necessary but not sufficient to prove that construction and lifecycle paths produce no unauthorized physical effects.

The authorised baseline contains the following directly observable composition:
ActuatorManagementService
Registers an actuator through the factory

ActuatorFactory
Constructs GPIORelay

GPIORelay.init()
Initializes GPIO mode and configures an output pin

Potential physical interface side effect
Without calling ordinary turn_on() or send_command()

Pinned baseline evidence:
- gpio_relay.py L27–42: GPIO configuration occurs inside the constructor.
- factory.py L62–103: the factory instantiates that adapter.
- actuator_management_service.py L418–424: the management service uses this factory.
The GPIO constructor contains:
self.GPIO.setmode(self.GPIO.BCM)self.GPIO.setup(self.pin, self.GPIO.OUT)



This establishes an interface-configuration side effect. Whether it actually changes a relay's physical output depends on the device, wiring, electrical state and platform behaviour. No such physical effect has been reproduced.

Counterexample: A synthetic R1a qualification instruments only turn_on(), turn_off() and send_command(). An inhibited or non-owner process nevertheless constructs a GPIO adapter that changes pin configuration. The command counters remain zero.

The test could satisfy its narrow oracle while failing to establish the broader passive-construction invariant.

Required correction:
1. Extend the mandatory containment oracle to all consequential physical-interface effects, not only explicit command submission.
2. Include constructor, registration, initialization, discovery, reconnect, disposal and cleanup paths.
3. Require synthetic instrumentation at the lowest relevant adapter or hardware-interface boundary.
4. Permit initialization affecting physical state only under separately defined ownership, recovery and device-specific safety contracts.
5. Extend FT-001, FT-016, FT-025 and the R1a/R1b acceptance criteria accordingly.
This does not require a new runtime architecture or immediate GPIO remediation within G0.2. It requires an explicit acceptance requirement so subsequent implementation cannot pass on incomplete evidence.

I verified that the candidate preserves all 34 previous fault-test identifiers, incorporates the 19 requested refinements and introduces FT-035–042.

These are substantial improvements. Nevertheless, two additional deficiencies remain.
G0.2-SS-02 — Combined recovery, transport and emergency faults
Severity: Medium

FT-025, FT-033, FT-034, FT-036, FT-039 and FT-041 cover important individual failure conditions. They do not explicitly require their combined qualification across protection, persistence, transport and ownership boundaries.

Counterexample:
1. A previously authorized operation remains outstanding in a simulated transport.
2. Independent protection intervenes.
3. The runtime terminates before completing reconciliation.
4. A replacement runtime encounters restored recovery data that predates the intervention.
5. Physical observation currently indicates a safe state, but the outstanding transport operation has not been reliably excluded.
A safe observation at that instant is insufficient to establish that rearm is permitted.

The architecture's proposed invariants prohibit unsafe recovery in this scenario. However, independent success of the individual fault tests would not necessarily demonstrate the combined invariant.

Required correction: Extend the cross-package fault-test matrix to require combined recovery scenarios. Specifically assert that emergency inhibition, invalidated authority and unresolved transport effects survive process replacement and persistence restoration. Rearm must not clear quarantine while an earlier consequential effect remains possible.
G0.2-SS-03 — Service-principal authorization coverage
Severity: Medium

Pinned evidence: D0-CONTRACTS.md L10–17 · SECURITY.md L13–17 · REMEDIATION.md L40–42.

The target contract appropriately distinguishes authenticated human principals from explicitly authorized service identities.

However, the fault-test matrix concentrates on anonymous connections, unauthorized resources, changed associations and revocation. It does not expressly require negative authorization cases for each internal command origin acting under a service identity.

Counterexample: A legitimate scheduler or controller possesses authority to perform a narrowly scoped operation. It subsequently receives work outside its permitted resource or action scope. Authentication succeeds, and the resource exists, but the particular service principal is not authorized for the requested effect.

The system must deny that operation even if runtime ownership, device availability and ordinary safety checks otherwise succeed.

Required correction: Extend FT-018 and FT-027, or add equivalent designs, to cover:
- Missing, disabled and revoked service identities.
- Authenticated but insufficiently scoped internal services.
- Scheduler, controller, calibration, ML and background-worker authority.
- Stale policy and configuration revisions.
- Conflicting resource permissions within shared-resource operations.
- Rejection before consequential submission, with no implicit privileged identity fallback.
EP-007 should retain ownership of identity and resource permissions; EP-003A should enforce those decisions at the mandatory admission boundary.
