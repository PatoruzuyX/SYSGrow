# SYSGrow engineering instructions

Status: G0/G0.1/G0.2/G0.3 candidate; repository governance becomes effective only through explicit owner-approved integration of the qualified revision. G0/G0.1/G0.2/G0.3 authorize documentation only. No ADR acceptance, runtime remediation, deployment or production activation follows from integration. Record the accepted governance commit, PR, approving owner and review/CI evidence in the integration record; see [G0 review record](docs/qualification/G0-REVIEW.md).

## Mandatory reading and authority

- Read this file, [engineering guidelines](docs/guidelines.md), [security policy](SECURITY.md), [architecture baseline](docs/architecture/FOUNDATION_BASELINE.md), [decision register](docs/architecture/adr/README.md), [findings](docs/qualification/FINDINGS.md), and the applicable [ExecPlan](docs/plans/SYSGROW-FOUNDATION-001.md) before editing.
- Explicit owner authorization is the authority for work. Accepted ADRs govern architectural choices; proposals and roadmap entries do not authorize implementation. If instructions conflict, stop and report the conflict. Source is evidence of current behaviour, not automatic approval of it.
- Tool-specific guidance in [.github/copilot-instructions.md](.github/copilot-instructions.md) and [.codex_profile.md](.codex_profile.md) supplements these rules only within the explicitly authorized task. Historical commands and examples are not authorization. Repository-wide rules, security gates, applicable accepted ADRs and the authorized ExecPlan constrain tool profiles; no incidental cleanup or scope expansion. If those authorities conflict, stop and obtain owner resolution; do not select the least restrictive instruction. Retain frontend design tokens, both themes, accessibility and existing JS hooks. Verify historical paths and commands before using them.
- Keep SYSGrow edge-first and independently usable without K.I.T.T., cloud, or external AI. Do not add new infrastructure or rewrite working subsystems without an accepted ADR and bounded authorization.

## Working rules

1. Pin remote main and compare the authorized baseline. Inspect local status, branches and worktrees when available; never assume a clean environment. Stop on material scope conflict, unknown ownership or unexpected changes. Remote-only sessions must disclose missing local evidence.
2. Use a dedicated branch/worktree and a bounded ExecPlan with findings, exclusions, tests, rollback and review gates. Never force-push, delete historical branches, alter repository settings, merge or deploy without separate owner authorization. Do not modify unrelated work.
3. Preserve previous completed functionality, API compatibility and historical evidence; reconcile backlog items before reopening them. Prefer existing services, domain contracts and repositories over duplicate owners.
4. Treat physical operation, broker/firmware changes, migrations, data access, credentials and external provider calls as explicitly gated. Use synthetic data, isolated temporary databases and simulated devices by default. Read-only, documentation and synthetic software qualification prohibit real-device operation and live private data access. Controlled device-specific hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) qualification requires separate explicit owner authorization, competent device-specific assessment, controlled equipment, established safety limits and a bounded qualification plan with stop/recovery criteria. HIL is not production activation or permission to use live private data. G0.3 remains documentation-only.
5. All ordinary consequential physical-interface effects (including construction, registration, initialization, discovery, reconnect, disposal and cleanup, not just commands) must enforce applicable ownership, resource/action authorization, recovery and deterministic device-specific safety boundaries; independently qualified protective mechanisms and narrowly authorized emergency recovery remain distinct; these are required target invariants, not claims of current compliance. No external AI safety override. Unknown physical outcomes are not success. See SAFE-001–015 in the foundation baseline.
6. Preserve current safeguards. Do not activate expanded autonomous, shared-resource or external physical-control features while Critical findings remain unresolved. A successful software test does not certify hardware.
7. Run applicable lint, format, tests and security checks; report commands, exact revision, outcomes, skips and blockers. Tests must exercise real service composition, negative paths and fault conditions where relevant. No fabricated evidence.
8. Obtain independent review of the exact candidate HEAD, resolve review findings, verify CI and diff scope, then request separate integration approval. Missing mandatory gate evidence, unresolved defects that invalidate a checkpoint invariant, and incomplete required independent review cannot be waived as ordinary residual risk. Out-of-scope baseline risks may remain open under documented owner disposition, containment and activation restrictions. Any changed HEAD, including evidence-only changes, requires fresh applicable exact-HEAD evidence and independent requalification. Record approval and post-integration closeout in the PR history, not by mutating the reviewed candidate. No automatic merge. Deployment and activation require separate qualification and approval.

## Closeout

Report baseline and final HEAD, changed paths, finding dispositions, tests and evidence, unresolved risks, decisions made without explicit guidance, review and integration status. Never close findings merely because documentation or a plan exists.
